How SSTV works

Slow-scan television turns a picture into sound: every pixel becomes a tone between 1500 Hz (black) and 2300 Hz (white), sent line by line with a 1200 Hz sync pulse marking each new line. A short VIS header at the start announces the mode — code 8 means Robot36, a 320 × 240 colour picture that takes about 37.7 seconds to sing. Because the picture lives in sturdy analogue tones, it survives noisy rooms and cheap speakers that would destroy ordinary data.

How Audio Cipher works

Your voice is stored as ordinary digital audio, then encrypted with AES-256-GCM — the same authenticated encryption used by banks and secure messengers. The key is derived from your password with PBKDF2 (100,000 rounds of SHA-256) plus a fresh random salt and IV for every message, all of which travel inside the coded WAV itself. The resulting sound is the ciphertext: indistinguishable from noise, and if even one byte changes — or the password is wrong — decryption refuses instead of guessing.
